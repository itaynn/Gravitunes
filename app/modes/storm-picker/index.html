STORM PICKER / SOUND LAB ON THE MOVING MAP

Pick it,
play it.

The moving NASA rain and snow map waits on its first frame. Move over it to listen: a soft pulsing tone tells you where you are (left and right is which ear, up and down is the pitch), and storms sound their rain or snowstorm, as loud as the storm is big and heavy. Click storms to pick spots; type a spot's number to drop it. Then press Space: the day plays once, the storm sounds stop, and every pick drifts with its storm through the Sound lab mapping below. A spinning storm adds vibrato (faster spin, faster vibrato; taller spiral, wider), a spreading storm adds overtones and a gathering one adds undertones.

01 / MAPPING: WHICH INPUT DRIVES EACH SOUND

Rain and snow each have their own sound. Pick the tab to edit it: its own key, scale, instrument and ranges. Every voice plays both sounds, mixed by how much snow it is sitting on, so crossing from rain into snow fades one sound into the other (set how gradual under Global sound). With Notes: Flowing, sustained instruments hold one continuous tone that glides with the map, and struck ones ring each time the voice reaches a new note.

Shape: Normal maps the input's low end to From and its high end to To. Inverted flips that. Strength ignores direction, so a fast storm moving west counts the same as a fast storm moving east. Calmness is the opposite of Strength. Notes sets how often a voice strikes, on a tempo grid; Chord stacks notes on top of the pitch.

03 / GLOBAL SOUND

Speed and spin come from how the colored patches move between frames, scaled to how active the map is right now, so the middle of a bar means still. Color follows the NASA legend: rain runs from green to red, and snow from cyan to purple. Your settings are saved in this browser.
