## Challenge

NASA’s Earth Information Center (EIC) presents visualizations of how Earth is changing. This challenge asks participants to make that science more accessible and engaging by translating visual information into sound. Build an “Earth Jukebox”: an interface, script, or application that pairs EIC visual material with generated sonification.

## Background

EIC visualizations show phenomena such as changing global temperatures and ocean currents, but primarily communicate through sight. Sonification—the use of sound to represent data—can offer blind and low-vision people another way to experience the information, reveal patterns, and give the public a multisensory connection to Earth science.

Possible approaches include analyzing visual frames and mapping image features to audio. For example, color could influence chords, pixel density could influence rhythm, or image shapes could control synthesizer parameters. These are examples rather than requirements; teams are encouraged to develop their own blend of software, data processing, and audio design.

## Objectives and possible deliverables

Create an experience that generates sonification from EIC visual frames, allowing people to choose or experience a visualization alongside its soundscape. Possible deliverables include:

- An interactive tool that translates visual data into sound.
- A web-based jukebox, desktop application, or backend API.
- A curated playlist or gallery of original generated audio, accompanied by the imagery and an explanation of the creative process.

Teams may use computer vision to analyze colors, shapes, or pixel densities, then map those features to pitch, tempo, instrumentation, or other audio parameters. Machine learning, open-source synthesis frameworks, and custom artistic rulesets are all possible. The challenge leaves the method open and encourages balancing data interpretation with musicality.

## Considerations

- **Sonification:** Use non-speech audio to convey or make data perceptible.
- **Visual frames:** EIC visualizations may be animations or images; extracting individual frames can provide input for audio generation.
- **Experience and accessibility:** Make visualization selection and playback intuitive. Consider screen-reader compatibility, tactile feedback, and descriptive text for blind and low-vision users.
- **Visual-to-audio mapping:** Candidate features include color, brightness, geometry, and spatial pixel density; candidate audio parameters include pitch, rhythm, tempo, volume, and instrumentation.
- **Data and music:** Direct pixel-to-sound mappings may be chaotic. Artistic rules, scales, or harmonies can help create an engaging result.
- **Storytelling:** Consider how the sound can communicate the urgency of climate change or the beauty of Earth's interconnected systems.
